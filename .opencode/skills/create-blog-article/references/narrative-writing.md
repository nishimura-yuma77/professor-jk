# Narrative Writing

Use this reference to make a Blog article feel composed rather than assembled. It provides principles, not a mandatory plot or section template.

## Core Principle

Narrative is a continuous change in the reader's attention, understanding, or feeling. It does not require a dramatic incident, chronological diary, conflict, or triumphant resolution.

The article should feel as though one mind is noticing, selecting, and arranging the material for one reason. Facts remain facts, but perspective, sequence, emphasis, rhythm, and omission turn them into an authored piece.

## Governing Intention

Privately define one governing intention before writing. It describes the movement the article should create in the reader, not merely the information it contains.

A useful private formulation is:

> By looking through [particular lens], move the reader from [initial perception] toward [changed perception].

Use this only as a thinking aid. Do not force the sentence into the published article.

A governing intention is not:

- A topic such as "API key storage."
- A table of contents.
- A generic goal such as "explain clearly."
- A moral attached after the draft.
- A requirement to make every detail support a predetermined conclusion.

The intention may remain exploratory. An article can move from certainty toward doubt, from complexity toward a useful distinction, or from an isolated implementation detail toward a wider question.

## Discover The Narrative In The Material

Look for energy already present in the supplied material:

- A question that changed what the author looked for.
- A detail whose meaning became clear only later.
- Two ideas that appear compatible but pull in different directions.
- A limitation that shaped the work more than the chosen technology did.
- A repeated image, phrase, action, or concern.
- A gap between the expected and observed result.
- A change in scale, distance, confidence, or responsibility.
- Something unresolved that gives the current state meaning.

Do not add a false obstacle merely because it resembles a conventional story beat. If the material is quiet, let close observation, juxtaposition, or gradual recognition provide the movement.

## Explore Directions Silently

Before drafting, consider two or three ways the same material could become an article. For each direction, imagine:

- Where the reader enters.
- What controls the order of revelation.
- What remains in the foreground and what becomes context.
- How the voice relates to the subject: intimate, reflective, observational, analytical, playful, restrained, or another justified stance.
- What kind of ending would feel earned.

Choose the direction that is most specific to the material. Prefer an imperfect but distinctive composition over a polished generic structure.

Possible organizing movements include chronology, inquiry, contrast, accumulation, reversal, a recurring motif, movement between detail and system, or returning to an opening image with changed meaning. These are possibilities, not templates. Combine or ignore them when the material suggests a better form.

## Compose Sections As Movements

Give each section one local intention. A section may establish, reveal, test, complicate, narrow, widen, interrupt, connect, or reframe. Its intention should be expressible as an action, not just a subject label.

For every section, know privately:

- What understanding or feeling the reader brings into it.
- What changes while the reader is there.
- What question, image, implication, or momentum passes into the next section.

Continuity does not require explicit transition phrases. It can come from cause and effect, a repeated word with changed meaning, contrast, an unanswered question, a shift in scale, or the consequence of the preceding observation.

Avoid resetting the article at every heading. A heading should not make the next section feel like a separate mini-article.

## Write With An Authored Voice

An authored voice comes from attention and choice, not decoration.

- Prefer details that could belong only to this article.
- Let interpretation follow concrete material when possible.
- Vary pace according to significance: linger where meaning changes and compress routine explanation.
- Use technical detail when it sharpens the reader's perception, not to prove completeness.
- Allow uncertainty to remain visible when the source material is uncertain.
- Use first person when it carries actual observation, judgment, or responsibility. Do not manufacture intimacy.
- Let humor, metaphor, or imagery emerge from the subject. Do not add them as a style layer.

Avoid explanatory boilerplate such as announcing the article, previewing every section, repeatedly stating that something is important, or summarizing immediately after showing the point.

## Use ArticleBlocks Without Fragmenting The Prose

ArticleBlocks are containers for the finished composition.

- Paragraphs carry the primary voice and movement.
- Headings mark meaningful changes in that movement.
- Lists compress genuinely parallel material; they should not replace developed thought.
- Code makes a mechanism, contrast, or consequence concrete.
- Images create evidence, atmosphere, scale, or a change of pace.
- External links extend the article without outsourcing its central reasoning.

Read across Block boundaries while editing. A technically valid sequence can still feel fragmented if each Block introduces itself from zero.

## Editorial Passes

### Intention

Can the article's governing intention be stated without listing its sections? Does every substantial passage contribute to that movement?

### Section Purpose

Does each section perform one distinct action? Are any two sections interchangeable, repetitive, or merely grouped by topic?

### Continuity

Does each section receive something from the previous one and leave something for the next? Do headings deepen the path or interrupt it?

### Voice

Could the prose have been generated for any article on the same broad topic? Replace generic framing with particular observation, implication, and judgment.

### Restraint

Remove facts included only for completeness, metaphors included only for color, transitions that explain an already visible connection, and conclusions already earned earlier.

### Resonance

The ending should not close the article mechanically. It may answer the opening, alter its meaning, widen the question, crystallize an image, or identify an honest present position. It should feel inevitable in retrospect but not interchangeable with the ending of another article.

### Truth

Creative composition never licenses invented experience. Verify scenes, chronology, feelings, motives, quotations, measurements, failures, and outcomes. When the material does not support a claim, omit it, qualify it, or ask the user.
